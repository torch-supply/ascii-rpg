# Content & tuning — notes

Detail for `src/content/**` — levels, monsters, items, config dials, lore, mutators. `levels.ts` is the spine (see the root `CLAUDE.md`). Tuning claims below were measured with the balance harness (`npm run test:play`); read `scripts/CLAUDE.md` for what that harness can and can't see before trusting a number.

## Loop, goals & pacing

- **Six goal types**: reach / collect / find / kill-boss / **cull** N (`killCount` — supported but unused; the Iron Gate moved to kill-boss so its big map gets traversed) / **survive** N turns vs an **escalating siege** (`maybeReinforce` spawns growing waves from a ring around the player, so you must fight/kite, not turtle). Two load-bearing details in that cap: it counts **combatants only** (`combatantCount` — ambient wildlife is placed outside `monsterBudget`, and letting the Ramparts' 3 ravens hold 3 of the slots throttled the siege; under Restless Dead, 21 combatants + 3 birds vs a flat 22 killed it outright, spawning zero waves), and it's a **floor, not a ceiling**: the effective cap is `max(CONFIG.siege.cap, monsterBudget + siege.headroom)`, so a denser level or trial gets real room to escalate into rather than being pinned at spawn. Reinforcements also roll for elite status like the garrison does (off the gameplay RNG), which is what lets Champions reach them at all, and spawn only on tiles WALKABLE from you (`walkableFrom` in `grid.ts`) — the ring is a Chebyshev band, so a wave used to land behind shut doors (inside the Ramparts' door-gated vault) and hold a cap slot for the rest of the siege. **Careful: on a survive level `monsterBudget` is not a difficulty dial in the obvious direction** — lowering it _raises_ difficulty, because the cap is the real governor and ring-spawned reinforcements arrive already in `chase` state, unlike the scattered idle garrison they replace (measured: budget 14→11 dropped the bot from 33% to 17%). Tune `siege.cap`/`headroom` or `goal.turns` instead.
- **Pacing:** the turn limit is a **par-for-score** target, NOT a threat. Only `survive` levels show a gauge (there the clock _is_ the goal); elsewhere there's no countdown and no overtime, and clearing under par grants an end-screen **efficiency bonus** (`levelParBonus` → `PlayerState.parBonus`). Running long costs nothing.
- **Arc:** dungeon → blackwood → mire → frostspine → **Blackhall Castle** (iron gate → great hall → sunken crypt → ramparts → dusk antechamber) → throne. Monsters toughen, light dwindles, par tightens.

## Economy & shop

- Between-level shop (9 tiers, one per level with a `shopTier`; buy + **sell** spare bag gear at `CONFIG.sellRate` × `sellPrice(def)`). **`ammo` is not in `SELLABLE`**, and re-adding it re-opens a money loop: ammo is the only category bought in BUNDLES (`ItemDef.value` = arrows per bundle), so a listing is 8g for twelve while `sellBagItem` pays per single arrow — and `sellPrice`'s `Math.max(1, …)` floor means no `baseValue` is low enough to close the gap (even 1g apiece sells a bundle back for 12g against 8g paid). Selling ammo needs a stack-at-bundle-rate sale, not a category flag. Test `[63]` pins the general ratio.
- **The wraith's bleed is at `chance: 0.25`, not 0.5** — and the sweep that set it is the reason to be careful with any monster carrying `inflicts`. It stacked three multipliers (high base damage, `armorPierce`, and the heaviest DoT in the game at 3/turn × 4 = 12 vs poison/burn's 8), landing 5 + 12 on a chainmail Warrior while its own spawn table — ghoul, skeleton, goblin — landed **1**. A 40-seed isolation sweep showed the bleed was doing nearly all of it: deleting it took the Great Hall 53% → 93% and the Crypt 40% → 70%, where shaving base damage 6→5 bought only +4. Two lessons beyond the number. **A DoT dwarfs the hit that delivers it** — 12 armour-ignoring damage over 4 turns is worth more than any per-hit tuning, so `inflicts` is the first dial to check on an over-performing monster, not the last. And **the balance harness cannot see `armorPierce` at all**: the bot wears the Warrior's starting leather (reduction 1), so `max(0, 1−2) === max(0, 1−1)` and pierce 2 vs 1 are byte-identical in effect. That row reading "no change" is the test being blind, not the dial being inert — measuring it needs a bot in real armour.
- **The balance harness barely sees `lootScale` or `coinPile`** — the two dials named above as the primary economy knobs. Gutting BOTH (coinPile 3–9 → 1, lootScale 0.67 → 0.2) moved the bot's whole-run purse 175g → 170g, while zeroing monster `coinReward` took it to 0 and reddened three checks at once. The bot beelines and fights, seeing only ~26–39% of a map, so ground gold is mostly never walked over and its income is almost entirely kill rewards. A row reading "no change" after a `lootScale` edit is the harness being blind to it, not the dial being inert — the same trap as `armorPierce` above. Ground loot has to be judged by placement and by play, not by `medGold`.
- **`runGold` is a sum of TWELVE-seed medians, so it is far noisier than it looks.** Across one afternoon of level-geometry work it swung 175–209g (15–18% of stock) while the same content at forty seeds held flat at 192–194g (16–17%). The affordability rail was set at 0.15, inside that noise, so it tripped on the sample; it is now 0.10, which is also the income band's own floor (120g / ~1174g) — at 0.15 an income of 120g would satisfy "stays near its tuned level" and simultaneously fail "can still afford meaningful purchases". Two guards on one quantity must agree.
- **WHAT gets hidden is a design decision, and it splits by purpose.** Ordinary loot used to scatter uniformly, so **49% of the game's items lay on open floor**: you collected them by walking the route you were walking anyway, and loot rewarded traversal rather than exploration. `placeItems` now biases to nooks — the bias `placeForage` and `placeLore` already used — but it **cannot apply to everything**. Hiding the lot took the carried-run gate from depth 3 to 2, because the balance bot beelines and never detours for loot, so it models a player who explores nothing, and that player starved. The split is by what an item is FOR: **treasure** (`coin`/`weapon`/`armor`) goes in the dead ends, because wealth should be the reward for looking; **supplies** (potions/ammo/torches) stay on the route, because running dry on heals for failing to search a corridor stub punishes the wrong thing. Measured after: treasure on open floor **49% → 5%**, supplies **92%** on the route, and whole-game placement open 49% → 23% / nook 16% → 35% / gated 15% → 23% / structure 20%.
- **Heals split by WHERE they come from, and only the exploration half is a hoard.** A run places ~19 heals, which sounds enormous against a 26 HP bar — but the number that matters is the split: **~8.4 come from the `dropTable`** (supplies place route-first, so a beeliner collects them and NEEDS them) and the rest from **`secretVault`/`structures` loot lists**, which cost a cracked wall or a hut door to reach. Cutting the route half is the obvious move and the wrong one: it starves the player who explores nothing, which is exactly the population the carried-run gate models, and that gate has no headroom. The exploration half was cut instead, 11 → 6.
  - The cut was possible because **every one of the six vaults contained a heal**, which is a variety problem before it is a balance one: breaking into a secret room always paid the same. Five became the scarcest thing in the game instead — a **situational draught**, matched to the level's own hazard (Mire → Rimewalk over its water, Frostspine → Levitation over its chasms, Iron Gate → Emberstep over its oil, Ramparts → Blinking off a linear wall-walk, Antechamber → Shadowcloak past the statues). Those five are `maxQty: 1` and only two-per-run purchasable, and **zero were findable anywhere before this** — so a vault now pays in the one currency the shop rations, and the lore vault hints have something worth following. Item COUNT per cache is unchanged, so exploration pays the same; what it pays in changed.
  - **The harness reads byte-identical across this change, and that is the expected result, not a null one.** The bot is not a looter and never breaks into a vault, so its win-rates and income cannot move — what the unchanged read verifies is the SAFETY RAIL (the beeliner's supply line and the carried-run gate are untouched), not the effect. The effect is on a player who explores, and nothing in `test:play` can see it. Verified instead by placement: all five draughts appear on 40/40 seeds and are gate-locked on 100% of them.
- **`lootScale: 0.6` is a measured floor, not a preference.** With the split in place, 0.67 and 0.60 both hold the carried-run gate at depth 3; **0.55 drops it to 2**. And that gate has NO headroom — its baseline is 2/2/3, so exactly one of three runs reaches the bar — which is why each step down was swept rather than guessed. The real leanness is placement, not count.
- **Three dials pushing one number is how you overshoot without seeing it.** Garrisons came down ~18% game-wide (146 → 120 monsters), so each remaining monster had to carry more. `coinReward` looked like the lever and was the wrong one: raised alongside richer drop tables AND higher `eliteChance`, run income hit **240g against a 193g baseline** — the opposite of lean. Backing `coinReward` out entirely landed at **188g with the carried-run gate improved** (2/2/3 → 3/2/3), because the other two were already doing the work: an elite pays double and drops guaranteed loot, so **raising `eliteChance` is itself an income raise**, and richer tables carry `c_gold`. Measure each dial before stacking the next.
- **Deliberately lean** so the shop is a real "what can I afford?" choice and attrition can cost a life. Tune the GLOBAL dials, not per-level counts: `CONFIG.lootScale` (0.6) scales ground drops, `CONFIG.forageScale` (0.5) scales wild-heal tiles (both applied in `generate.ts`, on top of any run-modifier tweak), `CONFIG.coinPile` (3–9) sizes gold piles, monster `coinReward` values stay modest, and shop heal stacks cap at `maxQty` 2.

## POIs & exploration

- **Altars** (`GameState.altars`, `LevelConfig.altarCount`, `src/game/core/altar.ts`) sit on **walkable** floor and never block a route — the offer opens AFTER you step on, so declining just walks you past. One pre-rolled bargain each (vigor: gold→+maxHP; **warblood**: −maxHP→permanent `PlayerState.weaponBonus`; hoard: HP→potions+gold); `applyAltar` is pure.
- **Lore props** (`GameState.lore`, `LevelConfig.loreCount`, text in `src/content/lore.ts`) — walkable `¶` glyphs placed by `placeLore` into NOOKS off the beeline (`openOrthoCount ≤ 2`). Step on an unread one to open a read-only `lore` mode (`LoreModal`; marks it `read` so it won’t reopen); no cost, no boon — the payoff is STORY. Each fragment is drawn from the pool of **the prop's own REGION**, not the level's base biome, so it speaks for where it lies and sub-region-only pools (`grove`/`undercity`/`cavern`/`ashen`) actually reach the player. Corollary enforced by test `[0]`: a fragment must not name a place its biome can appear OUTSIDE of. Flood levels protect lore tiles so an inscription never drowns unreadable.
- **Lore can give ADVICE, and advice must not lie** (`LoreEntry.claims`, test `[75]`). A fragment telling you how to fight something is only worth reading if it is right, and prose drifts silently: "frost cannot touch it" holds exactly as long as `MonsterDef.resist` says so, and changing that dial would leave the game lying to the player forever with nothing to catch it. Declaring the claim (`{ monsterId, resists }`) makes it checkable against `MONSTERS`, and also pins it somewhere the knowledge is USABLE — a warning about the troll is worthless on a level the troll never appears on, so the check requires the pinned level to spawn it, target it, or cage it as a vault guardian. Seven so far: a hunter's cairn warning that Gorm shrugs off cold (otherwise invisible — the only way to learn it is to waste a Frostbrand on him), and a VAULT hint on each of the six levels that hides one. That completeness is itself checked — a vault nobody hints at is a room you find by luck, and the clue system would otherwise be decoration on whichever levels happened to get written first.
  - **A vault hint never claims a position**, because the vault is placed per seed and a directional hint would be wrong most of the time. What it claims is authored and stable: the GATE (`{ gate, guarded }`), which is the actionable half — bashing through a cracked wall and hunting a shut door are different searches — plus whether something is caged in with the hoard. The check holds all three against `LevelConfig.secretVault`, so naming the wrong gate, inventing a guardian, or pointing at a level whose vault was removed all redden.
  - **`placeLore` seeds exactly ONE clue first**, and that is what makes advice worth writing. Drawn purely at random it barely arrived: the Great Hall's strongroom hint reached the player on **21%** of runs, the Frostspine's on ~40%. Seeding one takes that to **81-100%** while leaving room for flavour — at `loreCount` 2 the Frostspine would otherwise spend both props on advice and never show its atmosphere. Pinned at a 70% floor, which sits clear of the 21-40% "before" and the 81-100% "after". Per-vault reach now runs 64-99%; the Frostspine sits lowest only because it carries TWO clues that compete for the one seeded slot, so it still delivers _some_ advice on every run.
  - **A clue can never be a GATE.** Props are drawn at random from a pool larger than `loreCount`, so a given fragment reaches you on **22-89%** of runs by level (Mire 83-89%, Frostspine ~67% before this addition and 48% after, Iron Gate ~48%, Great Hall ~27%, Sunken Crypt ~25%). Requiring one would soft-lock most runs. Knowing is an advantage; not knowing costs a harder fight, never the run. A `LoreEntry.always` seeding flag would be the way to make a specific clue reliable if a level ever needs one.
- **A pool is keyed by BIOME, but two levels can share a biome and not be the same PLACE** (`LoreEntry.levels`). The Great Hall's new `crypt` undercroft and `ashen` collapsed nave exposed this: every `crypt` fragment had been written for the Sunken Crypt and narrated a flood ("toward the rising water", "when the crypt drowns") in a dry cellar two levels early, one of them naming the **Sunblade** before the game introduces it; both `ashen` fragments described the siege outside the gate — a slagged ram, burned fields — while sitting inside a cathedral. A fragment tied to a place now declares it, and the biome-general ones stay unpinned and serve both. Three guards, because every failure here is silent: a typo in a pin deletes a fragment from the game, a pin naming a level that lacks the biome is dead content, and — the subtle one — **the pool check has to be keyed by (level, biome) against the FILTERED pool**, since pinning every `crypt` fragment to the Sunken Crypt leaves `LORE_POOLS.crypt` six entries deep while the Great Hall's undercroft can draw none of them, and `placeLore` then just puts the props elsewhere on the level and loses the region's story with no shortfall to notice.
- **Ambient wildlife** (`MonsterBehavior "ambient"`, placed via `LevelConfig.ambient` OUTSIDE the combat `monsterBudget`) — they **drift** and **flee** within their shy radius (`driftAmbient`), never attack, and **disperse** harmlessly when bumped (no combat, coins, loot, or kill credit) — and by ANY other route too: a shot, Cleave, a firebomb, fire or haze scatters one the same way. `awardKill` is the choke point that refuses ambients, and `[77]` pins each route, because only bumping used to know the rule. Kept rare so they read as a treat: the **Will-o'-Wisp** (`wisp`, Blackwood — also a drifting light source via the opt-in `MonsterDef.glow`), **Carrion Ravens** (`raven`, Ramparts), **Mire Frogs** (`marsh_frog`), a **Snow Hare** (`snow_hare`, Frostspine). New species are pure data — an `ambient` `MonsterDef` + a `LevelConfig.ambient` entry.

## Run modifiers

- **Run modifiers** (`src/content/mutators.ts`) — opt-in seeded trials picked after class select (a `mutators` UI mode + `MutatorSelect` screen; `store.selectedMutators` → `beginRun`). `applyLevelMutators` runs in `beginLevel` BEFORE generation (so all generator guarantees still hold); `applyPlayerMutators` does run-start tweaks. Active ids live on `GameState.mutators`, roundtrip in the save, and raise the end score (`mutatorScoreMult`, shown on `RunStats` alongside `store.runResult`). Set: Dark Deepens / Restless Dead / Treacherous / Forsaken / Champions / Glass.
  - **A trial may only touch GENERATION-TIME config fields** (`baseLightRadius`, `monsterBudget`, `itemDropCount`, `eliteChance`, hazard/POI counts). `applyLevelMutators` runs at exactly ONE site — `beginLevel` — while ~30 other sites re-read the RAW `LEVELS[...]` entry (`goals.ts`, `levelParBonus`, `awardCoins`, `tickFlood`, the HUD, the renderer). Mutating `turnLimit`/`coinRichness`/`goal`/`flood` is therefore a **silent no-op you'd never see fail**; test `[40]` locks the touched-key set so a trial reaching for one reddens instead. The ONE mutator-aware runtime reader is the **siege** (`maybeReinforce`/`spawnWave` via `effectiveConfig`), which spawns monsters mid-level and so must honor the mutated `monsterBudget` + `eliteChance`. To make another runtime field respond to trials, route that site through `effectiveConfig` too — don't just widen the whitelist.
  - `champions`' `max(0.3, …)` **floor** is what makes it work at all on the three levels declaring no `eliteChance` (a bare `× 2` would leave the Pit/Blackwood/Throne untouched). `forsaken`'s `× 0.6` **compounds** with the global `CONFIG.lootScale` (0.67) applied later in `generate.ts`, so the count actually generated is ~0.40× of authored.

## Per-level signature beats

Every level carries one:

- **The Pit** (56×34) — a `cellular` **cavern** wing holding several glowing **fungal hollows** (`glowcap` pockets to navigate toward) + a door-gated cell.
- **The Blackwood** (84×52) — a boggy marsh patch, a bright **glade**, a thorn-choked **briar**, drifting **will-o'-wisps**, and a ruined woodcutters' **hamlet** of five varied huts with the shards scattered among them.
- **The Mire** (84×54) — water channels, dry grassy **hummocks**, a reed-thicket `maze` wing (green `♠` labyrinth, `organic` — see `src/game/core/map/CLAUDE.md`), a luminous **fungal grove** (`grove` — beautiful and poisonous), poison **spore vents**, and a lone mossy **wayshrine**.
- **The Frostspine** (72×46) — a `cellular` cave network choked with **crevasses**: frozen tarns (`water`, Rimewalk bridges them) AND bottomless **chasms** (`chasm`, Levitation only) — plus two **ice grottos**, a border **watchpost**, and a sealed **beast den** (a cracked-wall vault with a dormant Hoarfrost Bear over a Frostbrand — optional, so it's a risk you choose).
- **The Iron Gate** (84×50) — a `rogue` **gatehouse** of connected guard chambers whose goal is the **Gate Warden** held at the FAR end (`killTarget` places it farthest-from-start, so you fight ACROSS the map; a cull goal used to end in the spawn corner, leaving it unseen), opening onto an **ashen wastes** region (falling grey ash, ember-glow, pooled `oil` a firebomb turns to an inferno).
- **The Great Hall** — a `hall` **cathedral**: a colonnaded nave with flanking side-chapels, a **collapsed nave** open to the sky (an `ashen` patch where the roof fell in — ash drifting down, and the one place in Blackhall with daylight), an **undercroft** below (a `crypt`-biome `rogue` warren, first sight of what the castle keeps underground) and a door-gated **strongroom**. It was the sparsest level in the game at **1.11 POIs per 100 walkable tiles** because `genHall` floored **74%** of the map where every other level runs 18-40%: the chapels used to floor their entire depth to the map border, separated by single-column dividers. They are now rooms of jittered depth cut into solid rock with masonry behind them, and ~1 bay in 5 is never built — floor share **74% → 42%**, density **1.11 → 1.92**, with no threat added and no change in size. **Halving the walkable area doubles monster density at a fixed count**, which is the same incomplete edit as growing a map and not scaling its population: the budget came 20 → 16 (measured at 40 seeds, 20 read 40% and 16 read 68%; a strictly area-proportional cut to ~10 reads 80%+ and makes a cathedral full of the dead feel empty) — it has since been tuned to 13.
- **The Sunken Crypt** (60×38) — a catacomb `maze` wing + a bioluminescent **fungal hollow** (glowcap islands that stay dry and lit as the black water rises) + the **Blackhall underworks** (`undercity` — green-stained sewer stone and flooded channels, which is _where the flood comes from_), all of it **flooding** as you hunt the Sunblade.
- **The Ramparts** (84×34) — a `rampart` **wall-walk**: an exposed battlement over a bottomless `chasm` beyond a crenellated parapet (shove the dead through the embrasures), inner **tower rooms** to fall back to, lashed by a lightning **storm** and glazed with **ice**.
- **The Antechamber** (66×40) — a `gallery` processional **statue gallery**: a wide promenade flanked by a double **colonnade** with side aisles behind the pillars (flank / break LOS), patrolled by gargoyle "**statues**" (`guardChase` — stone until you draw near). Varied per seed: **niches** are per-bay randomized (~30% solid, the rest 1–2 deep, ~40% of deep ones cracked-wall-gated with an offering cache), and ~half of seeds drop a **collapsed** section (an interior `chasm` pit with a ragged eroded outline, kept off the spine so you route around it — but a gargoyle can shove you in). Plus a door-gated **reliquary** and a cosmetic `throne` region — black marble veining up through the floor, the throne's own stone spreading backwards, carrying its rising-ember atmosphere (it was the last level with no sub-biome at all).
  - **A `gallery` map is one rectangular band, and over half of it was untouched rock** (a ~17-row hall in 40 rows). So the level's variety problem was never density — it measured a healthy 2.02 POIs/100 walkable tiles — it was that every seed had the same PLAN: you entered mid-corridor, walked a uniform hall, and the Herald made its stand in a stretch identical to every other stretch. `genGallery` now spends that dead rock: a **narthex and apse** swell both ends a few rows above and below the band (arrival reads as a vestibule, the far end as an arena worth fighting in), and 2–4 **flanking chambers** are cut into the rock beyond the outer wall, each reached by one passage off a side aisle, with count/size/side/position all jittered — a reason to leave the processional axis. Both only ever ADD floor, so neither can sever the hall.
  - **The extra area was measured to be balance-neutral**: at 40 seeds the old and new geometry both read **35%**, because the new rooms are off the spine and the bot never enters most of them — so `monsterBudget` stayed at 14 while the POI counts scaled with the ~16% more walkable floor. Worth knowing for the balance pass: that 35% is the **lowest non-boss win-rate in the game**, and it was already so before the rework. The 12-seed gate reads this level at 33%, which is inside its own noise — a repeat of the Iron Gate lesson.

- **The Throne** (44×28) — kept tight for the boss.
