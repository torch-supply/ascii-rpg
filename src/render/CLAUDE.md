# Renderer internals

How the canvas layer is built and why. The root `CLAUDE.md` owns the one-directional data-flow
contract and the rule that React and the canvas are read-only consumers of state; this file is the
detail for `src/render/**` and `CanvasRenderer.ts`.

## Viewport & camera

Camera-based viewport: the renderer draws a window sized to the host at a fixed cell size (`TARGET_ROWS`) and scrolls to keep the player in view (edge-clamped) — the map may exceed the viewport.

## Lighting

Fog-of-war with **dynamic colored lighting** (`computeLightMap`, via `ROT.Lighting` — it lives in `src/game/core/light.ts` now, because it decides `state.visible`, and is re-exported through `src/render/lighting.ts`; what stays on this side is the per-biome AMBIENT tint, which colours a visible tile but never illuminates one. The renderer passes `now`/FX for flicker; the core calls the same function STEADY so visibility can't flicker at 60fps): multi-source light spreads through the FOV and bounces off surfaces — a flickering torch pool (dims + warm-shifts + gutters as `torchFuel` runs low; a **steady** faint eyes-only ember when unlit), per-tile-flickering fire, a gold Sunblade glow, cold-violet altars, teal **glowcap fungi** (bioluminescent grotto terrain that IS a light source — a dark `cavern` region navigable by its own glow), a boss aura, a pulsing red underlight on the lich's telegraphed barrage, and transient bolt/blast/hit flashes fed from the FX layer (`fxLights`). Each visible tile's glyph color is multiplied by (**per-biome ambient** + computed light) in the single `litVis` choke point; the ambient eases from a biome center-tint at the player to a ~fog-level edge floor (so a visible tile never reads darker than explored memory — no black ring at the vision rim). `explored` = darker memory, unseen = black; a throttled ambient rAF loop (`AMBIENT_MS`) drives the flicker + recomputes the light map; visible **water shimmers** and **marsh reeds sway** on the same loop (all off under `prefers-reduced-motion`, which also freezes the light steady). All lighting tuning (ambient tints, source colors, reflectivity, passes) lives at the top of `lighting.ts`.

## Set-piece lighting beats

**Set-piece lighting beats** modulate the per-region ambient off existing triggers (all in `renderBase`, cresting with the danger-music swell). Two use `LevelConfig.lightingBeat` with a gentle pulse, but DIFFERENT triggers (the levels are shaped differently): **`"dawn"`** (Throne) is keyed to Malachar's remaining HP — his multi-phase fight IS the level, so the hall warms + brightens toward a rekindled sunrise as his HP drains, cresting on the kill (the quest's payoff); **`"dusk"`** (Antechamber) is keyed to how far the player has **advanced toward the Herald** (the gate) — `1 − dist(player,boss)/dist(entry,boss)` — NOT his HP, because the Herald dies fast and the level then transitions, so an HP-keyed dusk would bottom out for only the instant before you leave; distance-keyed, the dim/cool deepens across the whole approach and peaks as you reach him. The color transform itself is the pure, unit-tested `beatAmbient(beat, g, ambient)` in `lighting.ts` (renderBase computes the eased intensity `g` and maps it over each region's ambient). The third: the **Sunken Crypt darkens + cools** as its flood rises (`floodStep`).

## Cosmetic FX

Cosmetic FX (via `effectBus` → `CanvasRenderer.playEffects`): `projectile` (head + fading trail), `hit` (white→ember spark), `blast` (expanding ring of sparks over the firebomb/Ruin radius) are canvas-drawn on the fx rAF loop; `damage` spawns a DOM `.dmg-num` that floats up and fades (red toward the player, gold toward monsters; anchored via the canvas's `offsetLeft/Top`; skipped under reduced-motion).

## The overlay canvas

A second **transparent overlay canvas** sits exactly atop the rot.js canvas (created in the renderer's constructor, sized/positioned + scrolled in lockstep each `paintOverlay`) and carries the effects the cell grid can't: (a) additive **bloom** (`paintBloom`) — soft radial-gradient halos on the brightest sources (fire, the Sunblade, glowcap fungi, in-flight bolts) blended with `globalCompositeOperation="lighter"` so they radiate; plus **per-biome atmosphere** (`BIOME_ATMOSPHERE` in `tiles.ts` → `paintAtmosphere`): drifting mist blobs (marsh/crypt), snow (mountain), rising embers (throne), dust motes (castle), drifting glow-spores (cavern) — procedural from the clock, off under reduced-motion. A level may also set **`LevelConfig.weather`** to override its base-biome atmosphere: **`"rain"`** = gentle falling rain streaks (the cursed **Blackwood**), **`"storm"`** = heavier rain PLUS periodic **lightning** flashes (`paintLightning` — a clock-driven white flash over the visible scene, main strike + aftershock; gated on `"storm"` only) on the exposed **Ramparts**. Both map through `WEATHER_ATMOSPHERE`. Bloom + atmosphere are then clipped together to the visible FOV set (one-fill `destination-in` in `clipToVisible`) so nothing bleeds past walls or into unseen tiles; and (b) cracked-wall **fissures** — `drawFissure` builds one crack as a TEMPLATE in unit space (a long main split through the origin plus 2–3 branches peeling off it, every line a polyline with perpendicular jitter so it wanders like a real crack), then rotates, scales and offsets the whole assembly by a per-tile seed. The origin is never drawn — it's just where the branches meet. Stroked in the wall's OWN colour darkened by `CRACKED_WALL_CRACK_DIM`, so it reads as a shadowed fracture through the glyph (needed because rot.js allows only one fg colour per cell), and deterministic per tile so it never flickers. A **knockout is already what this is**, incidentally: the stroke colour computes to within ~10–16 perceptual distance of the page black (`#131110` vs `#0d0d0d`), so covering glyph pixels with it is indistinguishable from erasing them — and true masking isn't available anyway, since the glyph lives on the rot.js base canvas which rot.js repaints on its own dirty-cell tick. Filled **spall chips** (flakes bitten out of the glyph) were built and removed: they rendered correctly — ~62% of chip area landed on glyph ink, removing ~15% of it — but at a 12–30px cell (`TARGET_ROWS` 26) a `#` has ~2px strokes, so a 15% bite is a 2–4px nick that reads as noise rather than as damage. **That's a perceptual floor, not an implementation gap**: you can't sculpt legible detail into a 2px stroke, so the only channel with enough resolution to say "damaged" at this size is the glyph SILHOUETTE — which would mean giving up the deliberate subtlety above. Don't re-litigate the chips without confronting that. That works because the stroke colour computes to within ~10–16 perceptual distance of the page black (`#131110` vs `#0d0d0d`), so covering glyph pixels with it is indistinguishable from erasing them. **True masking isn't available**: the glyph lives on the rot.js base canvas, which rot.js owns and repaints on its own dirty-cell tick, so a `destination-out` erase there would be raced and overwritten. Covering on the overlay is the equivalent, and is only equivalent because the cell background is a known flat colour — which is also why cracked walls must NOT get a bg tint. Two implementation details are load-bearing: the transform is applied to the POINTS, not via `ctx.scale`, because scaling the canvas scales the stroke and the hairline weight is the whole look; and the template is drawn LARGER than a cell and clipped to it, so cracks run out to the boundary and read as continuing into the surrounding stone (91% of tiles reach the edge) — without that clip the overspill lands on neighbouring floor tiles, measured at up to 10% of a cell. The strokes are deliberately **hairline with more splinters** rather than fewer heavy lines — the silhouette is what says "fractured stone". That trades away width, so the visibility has to come from DEPTH: the dim factor is 0.14, not the original 0.25, which players reported as too subtle to notice on the dim castle walls (and fog halves it again). Test `[54]` pins the crack-vs-wall contrast above 280 on every palette. A bg tint was tried here and **rejected**: a solid fill is a hard-edged rectangle on the cell grid, so it reads as a UI highlight rather than as surface texture — it looked like a selected cell, not damaged stone. The `chasm` gets away with a bg tint only because a chasm IS a featureless block of nothing.. The overlay sits _above_ the glyphs (the base canvas is opaque), so light is additive rather than occluding.

## Decals

**Persistent decals** (`GameState.decals`, index → `"scorch"`|`"blood"`; scorch stamped when a fire tile burns out, blood when a monster dies via `awardKill`; capped by `CONFIG.maxDecals`, which drops the OLDEST runtime stain via `GameState.decalOrder` and never evicts a region's generated ash — see `[78]`). **Ashen regions open already scorched** — `scatterRegionDecals` in `generate.ts` dusts ~22% of the region's walkable tiles with the `ash` decal from the map-gen stream (deterministic per seed, returned as `LevelData.decals` and seeded into state by `beginLevel`), because a burned region that differs from ordinary stone only in HUE stops registering within seconds; actual stains say _something burned here_. Runtime decals accumulate on top, and the pre-seed is kept well under the cap so kills and burnouts still leave marks. **It has to be `ash`, not `scorch`**: scorch is near-black (`#120d08`) and sits at a perceptual distance of just 56 from the ashen floor (`#241f1c`) — under the 110 bar `[54]` calls unreadable — so the first version of this feature was present in state and invisible on screen. Pale settled soot lifts off dark ground, and is the truer image for earth that already burned. All three are drawn on the overlay as soft **organic blobs** (`drawDecal` — a seeded rotated/squashed radial-gradient pool, plus splatter droplets and offset `lobes`), clipped to the visible FOV with the bloom. `DECAL_STYLE` in `tiles.ts` is the single source for every kind's colour/alpha/reach/drops/lobes: the renderer paints from it, `/style` enumerates it, and `[59]` asserts the ash tone clears the legibility bar _and_ that scorch would not have. Ash is deliberately the faintest by a wide margin — 3× under blood, 5× under scorch — because it blankets a whole REGION rather than marking one event, so it must sit beneath the terrain glyphs instead of competing with them. `lobes` exists because a single squashed gradient reads as a smooth _stamped_ blob; a couple of offset lobes give the silhouette bays and headlands so it looks settled. Droplets can't do that job — they're far smaller than the pool, so they read as specks beside a blob rather than reshaping it. **Alpha is a judgement no test can police** (`[54]`/`[59]` measure colour distance, not opacity), so changes there want an eye on `/style` and in game.

## Glyphs: one character, one meaning

`CanvasRenderer.ts` (viewport camera + fog + animation overlay), `Display.ts`, `tiles.ts` (glyphs/colors + biome variants, plus the color math everything else builds on: `mix`/`dim` and the legibility helpers `colorDistance` (redmean perceptual distance), `luminance`, `contrastRatio` — used by test `[54]` and the `/style` gallery). **Glyphs must stay unique across meanings** — a hazard and a boon sharing one is a bug (bramble was moved off `‡` because the altar uses it; the arcane forage mote off `∴` because the poison haze draws it; the wall sconce off `‼` because it is two of the potions' `!`; guttering fire off `*` because the collect-goal shards use it — it is now `▵`, the hollow twin of its own `▴` burning frame, so the two frames read as ONE flame flickering rather than as two unrelated shapes; and a SPRUNG trap off `^` to a dull `_`, because the armed and spent states shared `^` with the tint inverted — the harmless one wore the alarm red. `KNOWN_TRAP_GLYPH`/`ARMED_TRAP_COLOR`/`SPRUNG_TRAP_COLOR` live in `tiles.ts`, where `[54]` and `[69]` can see them). `/style`'s collision report is the by-eye check and test `[69]` is the mechanical one, the way `[54]` is for colour.

- **`[69]` keys on the CATEGORY set, not the glyph, and locks it in both directions.** A collision only lies when it crosses categories — twelve potions on `!`, seven weapons on `/`, a cracked wall drawing as the wall it hides in, a hidden trap drawing as floor are all conventions and fall out automatically. The first version listed allowed _glyphs_ and could not tell that fixing `*` had worked, because the two quest items still share it between themselves: the glyph kept colliding for a harmless reason and the entry looked live. Locking the set means a new cross-category collision reddens AND delisting is forced when one is fixed, so the list can't become a graveyard of accepted bugs. It cannot catch a NEAR-miss (`‼` vs `!` are different codepoints and compare clean) — that is what the gallery showing them side by side is for. It also only covers glyphs that PERSIST on the map: the blast sparks in `fxCells` still draw `*`, which is idiomatic for an explosion and lasts a few frames, so it is left alone deliberately rather than overlooked.
